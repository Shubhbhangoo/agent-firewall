# v2.1 Security Invariants

These invariants are the contract of the Autonomous Agent Defense Layer.
Every subsystem is designed so that violating one is harder than
describing it - and `test_v2_1_adversarial.py` enforces each one.

## The invariants

### 1. Identity does not equal authority

An active identity proves *who* an agent claims to be. It grants
nothing. The defense mesh evaluates identity and capability separately:
an agent with a verified identity but no live capability is restricted,
never trusted into action.

### 2. Credentials do not automatically become capabilities

Presenting a capability-shaped token - even one signed by a trusted
issuer - does not mean the presenter holds it. The mesh's capability
provider binds capability to agent through the SDK's own registry; the
a2a layer requires an explicit scoped relationship; the research lab's
`malicious_agent` scenario proves an attacker presenting another
agent's capability fails closed.

### 3. Delegation can only narrow authority

Every delegation mechanism intersects the grant with the parent's
effective authority:

- v1.x capability delegation: `_constraints_are_narrower` raises on
  widening at delegation time.
- v2.0 task delegation: child permissions are the intersection of the
  parent's effective permissions and the grant.
- v2.1 a2a delegation: child effective permissions are the intersection
  of the parent's effective permissions and the grant; `verify_chain`
  proves monotone narrowing.
- v2.1 capability2: `attenuate`/`delegate` produce structurally narrower
  policies and `is_narrower_than` is enforced.

### 4. Revocation propagates correctly

- Capability lineage: revoking a parent denies every descendant
  (`is_effectively_revoked` walks the chain).
- a2a relationships: revoking a relationship revokes every relationship
  derived from it (recursive, returns the count).
- Identity revocation: a revoked identity fails verification, mesh
  evaluation, and evidence signing (identity signers become
  unverifiable).

### 5. Simulation cannot mutate production state

The twin snapshots a serializable attack graph and works on deep
copies. It never holds a live registry reference and no counterfactual
can change production authorization state. Every counterfactual report
is labeled `simulated`.

### 6. Inference cannot become evidence without explicit provenance

Evidence kinds (`observed`, `inference`, `prediction`, `simulation`,
`unknown`) are structural. Promotion to `observed` requires an explicit
`promote()` call with a reason; the new event references the original
via `promoted_from` and the original is never rewritten. The immune
system's detections and advice are recorded as `inference` even when
they trigger real actions - the *action* is observed, the *analysis*
is not.

### 7. Model output cannot authorize itself

The immune system's reasoner (which may be an LLM) returns advice only.
Execution requires a deterministic `ImmuneRule` match, and high-impact
stages (`quarantine`, `contain`) require an approver unless the policy
explicitly sets `auto_approve`. An advice naming an action no rule
authorizes is skipped with an explanation.

### 8. Every security decision is explainable and auditable

- Mesh transitions carry an actor, a reason, a timestamp, and an
  optional signed attestation.
- A2A relationship changes are audited through the attestation
  authority.
- Immune actions carry the rule id, the reason, the outcome, and the
  evidence; executed actions are recorded as observed evidence.
- Evidence graph events carry payloads, causal parents, and signatures.

### 9. Security failures fail closed wherever authorization is concerned

Unknown agents, broken lineages, unverifiable evidence, malformed state,
expired recovery windows, and provider errors all deny. There is no
"we could not tell, so allow" path.

### 10. A delegated capability never gains authority compared with its parent

Capability2 `delegate`/`attenuate` are narrowing-only by construction;
`is_narrower_than` is checked after every operation.

## Enforcement map

| invariant | primary enforcer | adversarial test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `DefenseMesh.evaluate` | `test_identity_authority_invariant` |
| 2 | mesh capability provider, a2a authorization | `_attack_malicious_agent` |
| 3 | intersection semantics everywhere | `_attack_delegation_chain`, `_attack_capability_escalation` |
| 4 | lineage walk, recursive revoke, registry status | `_attack_revocation_bypass`, `test_revocation_propagation` |
| 5 | twin snapshots + deep copies | `test_twin_never_touches_live_graph` |
| 6 | structural kinds + explicit promotion | `test_inference_stays_inference` |
| 7 | immune policy rules + approver | `test_model_output_cannot_self_authorize` |
| 8 | signed/audited transitions | mesh/a2a/immune tests |
| 9 | fail-closed paths | `test_fail_closed_invariant` |
| 10 | `is_narrower_than` | `test_capability2_delegation_widening_narrows` |

## Non-guarantees

- v2.1 does not detect every intrusion; heuristics are labeled
  `inferred`.
- The twin's counterfactuals are simulations: predicted reach is not a
  recorded fact, and reachability is not exploitability.
- Nothing defends against a fully compromised issuer or signing key.
- An approved human action is the highest authority and is trusted.
