# v3.1 performance: what proving that an external system vouched for state costs

Methodology follows the suite's standard (see `firewall/benchmarks.py`):
warmup, then `repeats` timed calls reported as median and p95 of
`time.perf_counter`; `operations_per_second` derives from the median. All
figures below are from one development machine (CPython 3.10.4, Windows 10,
AMD64, `time_resolution` 15.6 ms, `perf_counter_resolution` 1e-07) and are
directional, not a benchmark. Run `python -m firewall.benchmarks
attestation` locally for current numbers.

## What is measured

v2.9 established that the firewall's own record could be trusted. It could
not establish that the *external system agreed with it*, because nothing in
the journal came from the external system. The `attestation` group measures
what closing that gap costs, on top of the layer below it:

| benchmark | what one operation does |
| --- | --- |
| `attestation_record` | authorize -> reserve -> start -> prepare -> attempt -> receipt, then one signed envelope is minted for that exact effect and presented: algorithm/version checks, a trust lookup, an Ed25519 verification, the scope comparison against the journal row, the correlation comparison against the receipt, the freshness window, the contradiction check, the nonce claim against the replay ledger, and one journal row |
| `attestation_commit` | the same, plus the v2.9 verification and a commit that *requires* attestation: the presented envelope is verified again at the gate, freshness is re-checked against the journal, and the lease is recorded `COMPLETED` |
| `attestation_unattested_commit` | the fail-closed refusal: the effect is recorded succeeded and verified, the deployment requires external evidence, none is supplied, and the completion is refused and journaled |
| `attestation_forged_record` | the refusal path for a genuinely signed envelope that attests a **different** effect: everything about it verifies and it is still refused |

Every row includes **minting the envelope** (one Ed25519 signature) inside
the measured region, because each operation needs a fresh statement bound
to a fresh effect. In a deployment that work happens in the attestation
bridge, outside the boundary; the firewall's own share of these numbers is
therefore smaller than the deltas below suggest.

## Results (directional)

| layer | median ms/op | p95 ms/op | ops/s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `effect_authorize` (reference: bare allow) | 0.33 | 0.35 | ~3,000 |
| `effect_receipt` (v2.8: through the observed receipt) | 1.89 | 2.12 | ~529 |
| `attestation_record` (v3.1: + mint, verify, correlate, journal) | 2.52 | 2.77 | ~398 |
| `effect_verify` (v2.9: + named verification) | 2.19 | 2.44 | ~457 |
| `effect_commit` (v2.9: verified completion) | 2.53 | 2.78 | ~395 |
| `attestation_commit` (v3.1: attested completion) | 7.51 | 8.41 | ~133 |
| `effect_unverified_commit` (refused: unverified evidence) | 2.21 | 2.54 | ~453 |
| `attestation_unattested_commit` (refused: attestation required, absent) | 4.92 | 5.14 | ~203 |
| `attestation_forged_record` (refused: envelope attests another effect) | 4.33 | 4.80 | ~231 |

Readings from the run that produced this table (20 operations per call, 1
warmup, 5 timed repeats; `effect_authorize` is 100 operations):

- `effect_authorize`: median 33.3 ms per 100-op call (p95 34.5 ms).
- `effect_receipt`: median 37.8 ms (p95 42.3 ms), min 33.4 ms.
- `attestation_record`: median 50.3 ms (p95 55.4 ms), min 47.9 ms -- about
  **+0.63 ms/op** over the receipt for the whole attestation stage.
- `effect_verify`: median 43.8 ms (p95 48.8 ms); `effect_commit`: median
  50.6 ms (p95 55.6 ms).
- `attestation_commit`: median 150.2 ms (p95 168.1 ms) -- about **+5.0
  ms/op** over a v2.9 verified completion. This row deliberately presents
  the envelope *twice* (once to `record_attestation`, once to
  `commit_effect`, which re-verifies whatever it is handed rather than
  trusting the caller), so it pays two Ed25519 verifications. A deployment
  that records the claim first and then commits with
  `attestation_required=True` and no envelope pays one.
- `attestation_unattested_commit`: median 98.5 ms (p95 102.7 ms) for a
  **refused** completion.
- `attestation_forged_record`: median 86.6 ms (p95 95.9 ms) for a
  **refused** attestation.

### A repeat run, and what it says about these figures

Re-running the `attestation` group on the same machine while it was busy
moved the rows by up to about 30%: `attestation_record` 2.52 -> 3.02 ms/op
and `attestation_commit` 7.51 -> 5.11 ms/op, with p95 gaps as wide as
`attestation_commit`'s (5.11 median against 7.41 p95). Nothing in the layer
changed between the runs. What that second run means for reading the table
above:

- The **stable** readings are the orderings and the ratio: the attestation
  stage costs well under a millisecond of boundary work on top of the
  receipt, and an attested completion costs roughly two to three times a
  v2.9 verified completion.
- The **specific** figures are a snapshot of one quiet run. Any claim built
  on a single pair of rows -- "the attestation stage costs 0.63 ms" -- is
  quoting a measurement whose spread is the same order as the value. The
  doc quotes it because a reader deserves the number that was actually
  taken, not because it is reproducible to two decimals.
- Run `python -m firewall.benchmarks attestation` yourself before quoting
  any of this in an engineering decision.

## Reading the numbers

- **Verifying one envelope costs well under a millisecond of firewall
  work.** The receipt-to-attestation delta is +0.63 ms/op on this machine,
  and part of that is the Ed25519 *signature* the benchmark mints inside
  the loop. What the boundary adds is one verification, one scope and
  correlation comparison, one freshness comparison, one nonce claim and one
  journal row -- the same order of magnitude as the v2.9 verification stage
  it sits beside (`effect_verify` costs +0.30 ms/op over `effect_receipt`
  on this run).
- **The full attested chain is the honest number for an operator.** The
  `attestation_commit` row is the price of a completion that rests on a
  statement signed outside the firewall: ~7.5 ms/op against ~2.5 ms/op for
  a v2.9 verified completion. That is roughly three times the layer below
  it, and the largest single contributor is that this row verifies the
  envelope twice by construction. The property bought is the release's
  whole purpose: the difference between "the firewall recorded an effect"
  and "the external system authenticated its state".
- **Refusing costs about what accepting costs, and that is the point.**
  The two refusal rows are the same order of magnitude as the accepting
  row. There is no cheap shortcut that answers "no" without doing the
  work: the forged envelope gets a real trust lookup and a real signature
  verification before the scope comparison refuses it, and the unattested
  completion runs the whole v2.9 verified chain before the attestation gate
  refuses it. A cheaper refusal would mean a weaker check somewhere
  upstream in the same call.
- **Spread is published rather than smoothed.** On a machine also running
  a test suite, p95 runs 6-12% above the median, and the difference between
  the two refusal rows and the accepting row is inside a band that large.
  A doc that read a precise overhead figure out of the gap between
  `attestation_record` and `attestation_forged_record` would be quoting the
  scheduler.
- **Nothing here may be used to weaken a check.** The legitimate levers are
  *where* the nonce ledger lives (a file an attacker cannot roll back),
  *how long* a deployment is willing to accept an attestation
  (`attestation_max_age_seconds`) and *whether* it requires one at all
  (`require_external_attestation`). The number that must never improve by
  making the layer cheaper is the one below it: a completion that rests on
  external evidence and cannot point at a verified statement is not a
  faster completion, it is a weaker claim.
