# v2.8: The side-effect commit protocol - explicit, attestable,
# idempotent, recoverable external effects

v2.7 closed the gap between ALLOW and the action with an execution lease
and stated the boundary its guarantee stops at in the same document:

```text
STARTED
   |
   v
external side effect        <- no in-process record can close this window
   |
   v
COMPLETED
```

The firewall does not own the external system. It cannot make an
uncontrolled external world behave transactionally, it cannot atomically
cover the instant between its `STARTED` transition and the moment the
handler's effect lands in the world, and it cannot roll an effect back.
v2.7's non-guarantees are unchanged and are repeated where relevant
below.

v2.8 does not pretend to close that window. It changes what the firewall
does **around** it, so that the boundary that v2.7 made *recorded* is
now **explicit, attestable, idempotent and recoverable**:

> A side effect must never be represented as successfully completed
> unless the firewall can establish what execution authority existed,
> what side-effect attempt occurred, and what completion evidence was
> observed.

That is the v2.8 property, and it is the sentence
`SIDE_EFFECT_COMMIT_INTEGRITY`, the nineteenth registered invariant,
machine-checks.

## 1. AUTHORIZED, EXECUTED, OBSERVED, COMPLETED

The four words the whole release turns on must not be blurred:

| Word | Meaning | Who establishes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AUTHORIZED** | the canonical boundary allowed the request | `FirewallSDK.authorize()` |
| **EXECUTED** | the lease left `STARTED`; the action may have run | the lease record (`executed=True`) |
| **OBSERVED** | an external outcome was reported for one attempt | the side-effect receipt, with its evidence kind |
| **COMPLETED** | the execution closed cleanly over established evidence | the lease record, only when all three above hold |

The critical v2.8 claims are the two separators that v2.7 could not
draw:

* **EXECUTED does not imply OBSERVED.** A lease stuck in `STARTED`
  (crash after the request, timeout, handler raised) says the action may
  have run. It does not say what happened, and it must never be promoted
  to `COMPLETED` on that basis alone.
* **OBSERVED does not imply COMPLETED under valid authority.** A receipt
  is an observation. When it arrives after authority was lost it is
  recorded truthfully *with its authority flag set `False`*, and the
  execution stops in an explicit terminal failure saying the effect
  happened but did not complete under currently valid authority. History
  is never rewritten because authority later changed.

And the whole protocol sits below `authorize()` in the same sense the
lease did: no receipt, no intent row and no effect digest ever grants
authority. `firewall/authorization.py::authorize` remains the only allow
origin, and `AUTHORIZATION_UNIQUENESS` still pins it.

## 2. What was added

### 2.1 The side-effect journal (`firewall/effect.py`, `firewall/effect_store.py`)

A second journal beside the lease journal. One external side effect per
execution lease - enforced by a unique constraint on `lease_id` - with
its own five-phase state machine:

```text
INTENT_RECORDED  --attempt-->  ATTEMPT_STARTED  --receipt/-->  SUCCEEDED
     |    (nothing was ever          |            reconcile     FAILED
     |     transmitted)              |                          UNKNOWN
     +-- FAILED (deadline passed     +-- reconcile ----------------+
          before any attempt)              |                       |
                                           v                       |
                              UNKNOWN --reconcile--> SUCCEEDED / FAILED
                              (re-stamp UNKNOWN when still unknown)
```

* **`INTENT_RECORDED`** is the durable outbox row. It exists *before*
  any external request can be authorized. Its deadline only lapses it to
  `FAILED` with `terminal_reason=effect_deadline_passed`, which the row
  itself justifies: it never left `INTENT_RECORDED`, so nothing was ever
  transmitted.
* **`ATTEMPT_STARTED`** is the boundary crossing: exactly one atomic
  compare-and-set per row, stamped with an attempt identifier. After it
  the journal says an attempt was authorized and initiated; it says
  nothing about what happened.
* **`SUCCEEDED` / `FAILED`** are confirmed terminal outcomes. Nothing
  leaves them, so a replayed receipt cannot produce a second completion
  and a contradictory later report is refused
  (`effect_already_resolved`).
* **`UNKNOWN`** is the explicit representation of external uncertainty.
  It is not terminal in the state-machine sense - an evidence-carrying
  reconciliation may resolve it to a confirmed outcome or re-stamp it -
  but it is terminal *for automatic progress*: nothing retries an
  unknown side effect, because a retry could duplicate a real-world
  action. The only way out of `UNKNOWN` is `reconcile_effect`.

Rows bind the security-relevant fields that exist in the v2.7
architecture: lease id, execution id, capability fingerprint, agent,
capability, action, request digest, effect type, effect digest,
idempotency key, chain id and fingerprints, policy version, and the
authority-epoch sample. The effect payload itself is **not** stored - the
row carries its canonical digest (`canonical_effect_digest`), so
sensitive effect data is not duplicated into the journal.

The journal is state, not evidence and not authority - exactly the lease
store's position. Keeping it a second table means an operator may throw
it away without rewriting history and archive history without resuming a
side effect.

### 2.2 The SDK protocol (opt-in)

Seven additive methods on `FirewallSDK`, every one verdict-shaped
(`EffectResult` with `allowed`/`reason`/`state`/`effect`, or an
`ExecutionLeaseOutcome` for the lease-level steps), never an exception:

```python
prepared  = sdk.prepare_effect(lease, cap, action, request,
                               effect=..., effect_type=..., idempotency_key=...)
attempted = sdk.attempt_effect(...)          # only from STARTED
receipt   = sdk.record_effect_receipt(...,
                               observed_outcome="succeeded"|"failed"|"unknown",
                               evidence_kind="caller_assertion"|
                                            "handler_observation"|
                                            "provider_evidence",
                               external_request_id=..., provider=...)
committed = sdk.commit_effect(...)           # ExecutionLeaseOutcome -> COMPLETED
recovered = sdk.reconcile_effect(..., resolution=..., evidence_kind=...)
outcome   = sdk.run_effect(..., handler=...) # one-call form
```

Construction takes `effect_journal=` or `effect_store_path=`. When the
execution lease store is already persistent (`execution_store_path=`),
the journal shares its file, so a restart recovers both journals from one
database; the tables are independent.

**Backward compatibility.** Existing v2.7 callers are untouched. The
protocol is opt-in: a caller that never calls `prepare_effect` sees
exactly the v2.7 behaviour, including the documented v2.7 non-guarantees.
The one coupling runs the other way: once a caller **has** prepared an
effect for a lease, the plain `complete_execution` on that lease is
refused with `effect_unresolved:<state>` until the effect is resolved by
a receipt or reconciliation - a caller cannot record a durable intent
and then bypass the protocol. The refusal leaves the lease in place for
recovery; it never guesses.

**What a caller who bypasses the protocol loses** is precisely the v2.8
property for that action: without an intent row there is no durable
outbox record, no idempotency key, no attempt identifier, and no
three-way outcome - so a crash after the request can only be represented
the v2.7 way (a `STARTED` lease, `executed=True` on abort), and a retry
of the same real-world action is indistinguishable from a new one.

### 2.3 Authority stays attached

Every journal progression re-establishes the execution's authority basis
against live state first, through the exact deny-only continuity
validation v2.7 runs on the lease (`_continuity_failure`): issuer trust,
signature, revocation including ancestors, lease and capability time
windows, delegation chain, policy version, authority epoch, Aegis
restrictions and risk state. Unreadable state is a refusal naming it;
nothing journal-shaped can advance under authority the execution cannot
still establish.

An observation that arrives **after** authority was lost - revoke,
policy change, epoch move, Aegis suspension, risk revocation, all landing
between `ATTEMPT_STARTED` and the receipt - is handled the only honest
way:

1. the receipt/reconciliation is still recorded truthfully (the effect
   happened; history is not rewritten);
2. the row's `receipt_authority_valid` flag is set `False`, so the row
   itself says the effect did **not** complete under currently valid
   authority;
3. the lease is burned to its terminal failure (`REVOKED`/`EXPIRED`/
   `DENIED`) with `executed=True`; and
4. `commit_effect` refuses (`effect_unresolved:<state>`), so nothing
   clean is ever completed over that observation.

### 2.4 Idempotency semantics - the legal combinations

The protocol makes the *firewall* idempotent even when the external
system is not (internal deduplication is exactly that, and the
non-guarantee that it cannot un-send an already-sent external request is
unchanged from v2.7). One lease carries one side-effect row, so:

| Caller presents | Result |
| --- | --- |
| same lease, same effect, same idempotency key (retry) | the same row is returned (`effect_already_prepared` / `effect_already_attempted` / `effect_already_resolved`); no second attempt is ever authorized |
| same lease, different effect or different key | `effect_mismatch` - one lease carries one side effect; a modified effect never executes under a recorded intent |
| different lease, same live execution id | refused at reservation (`execution_identity_bound`), so this combination cannot be constructed |
| same effect, different execution | a fresh side-effect row for the next execution - legitimate, and the reason idempotency keys are per-execution |

An omitted idempotency key defaults to the effect digest, so an
unprepared retry of the same logical effect naturally maps to the same
key.

**What the firewall cannot guarantee about the external system.** If the
external system provides its own idempotency key, pass it through and it
is preserved as correlation evidence on the receipt. If it provides none,
Agent Firewall can guarantee that its own representation authorizes at
most one attempt per row - it cannot guarantee that an already-sent
request was not processed twice by the external system, and it does not
pretend internal deduplication can undo an already-sent external request.

### 2.5 Receipts: observation, never proof

A receipt records what a handler claimed happened, bound to the
execution, the intent, the effect digest, the idempotency key and the
attempt identifier. The data model keeps three evidence kinds distinct:

* `caller_assertion` - the caller says its own call succeeded;
* `handler_observation` - the handler observed a response;
* `provider_evidence` - the provider's response was authenticated by the
  integration (an integration must actually authenticate the provider to
  use this label).

An external request/transaction id is preserved as `external_request_id`
when supplied - as correlation evidence, never as proof that the effect
happened, unless the integration contract actually establishes that.

The receipt does not decide anything by itself. `receipt != proof`, and
a receipt never restores an authority that was withdrawn (section 2.3)
and never advances another execution (section 4, attacks 8-9).

## 3. Recovery and the crash matrix

Recovery is built on the durable intent: after a restart the journal
still says what was intended, that an attempt was authorized, and that
no outcome was observed. The deterministic crash matrix:

| Crash at | State after restart | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before intent persistence | no row at all; lease `STARTED` | nothing was recorded, so nothing can be claimed |
| after intent persistence | `INTENT_RECORDED`, no attempt id | the outbox survives; nothing was transmitted |
| before the external request | `ATTEMPT_STARTED` with attempt id, no observation | an attempt was authorized and initiated |
| after the external request | `ATTEMPT_STARTED`, no observation | **the worst window**: the request may have been processed |
| after receipt persistence | the recorded `SUCCEEDED`/`FAILED`/`UNKNOWN` | receipts are persisted atomically |
| before COMMIT | row resolved; lease still `STARTED` | completion evidence exists; the caller (or operator) commits |
| after COMMIT | lease `COMPLETED` over the resolved row | completion was actually established |

No crash state silently reads as `COMPLETED`. `expire_lapsed_effects`
lapses only `INTENT_RECORDED` rows; an attempted row is never lapsed,
because deciding the external system did nothing is exactly the guess the
journal refuses to make. `UNKNOWN` is never auto-retried - the only
resolution path is the explicit `reconcile_effect`, which records
confirmed success, confirmed failure, or a re-stamped
"still unknown" with the reconciliation attempt in the row's history
(`reconcile_count`, `last_reconciled_at`).

## 4. The invariant: SIDE_EFFECT_COMMIT_INTEGRITY

Registered as the nineteenth invariant. It verifies, by machine:

1. **Source census (both directions)** - only the declared protocol
   methods on the SDK (`prepare_effect`, `attempt_effect`,
   `record_effect_receipt`, `reconcile_effect`,
   `expire_lapsed_effects`) drive the journal's mutators, and each of
   them does. A future developer who adds an
   `external_execute(...)` that writes the journal outside the protocol
   fails the gate; so does a declared method that stops driving one. The
   lease-level `commit_effect`/`run_effect` are deliberately not in the
   journal-mutator census: they read the journal and drive the lease
   completion, which the lease census already owns.
2. **State-machine algebra** - confirmed outcomes are irreversible
   (nothing leaves `SUCCEEDED`/`FAILED`, so a replayed receipt cannot
   produce a second completion), `UNKNOWN`'s only outgoing edges are
   explicit reconciliations (plus the re-stamp), and
   `is_terminal_effect` agrees with `TERMINAL_EFFECT_STATES`.
3. **Record hygiene and cross-journal checks** - every stored row ends
   where its history stops, enters a confirmed outcome at most once, and
   claims only what its evidence supports: `SUCCEEDED` carries a success
   observation and an evidence kind, `UNKNOWN` is never recorded as
   success or failure, `ATTEMPT_STARTED` carries an attempt id and no
   observation, `INTENT_RECORDED` carries neither. Each row is bound to
   a lease that exists, carries that lease's execution identity, and - a
   lease recorded `COMPLETED` that carries a side-effect row - shows the
   effect `SUCCEEDED` under valid authority.

`python -m firewall.invariants --exercise --strict` now reports
`19 invariants: 19 holds, 0 violated, 0 unverifiable`; the canonical
estate walks one side effect through prepare -> attempt -> receipt ->
commit alongside the existing executions.

## 5. What v2.8 cannot guarantee (unchanged, stated)

* **The external world is not transactional.** If the external system
  processed the request and the firewall only ever saw a timeout, the
  effect is `UNKNOWN` and stays `UNKNOWN` until an external-status query
  or an operator reconciles it. Agent Firewall guarantees what its own
  record claims; it cannot guarantee what an uncontrolled external system
  did.
* **Internal deduplication cannot un-send.** Exactly-once applies to the
  journal's *representation* (one row, one attempt, one resolution). An
  external system without its own idempotency key can still receive
  duplicate requests from two different executions of the same logical
  effect - those are two legitimate side effects.
* **The receipt is as strong as its integration.** `provider_evidence`
  is a label an integration earns by authenticating the provider's
  response. Agent Firewall does not verify external systems; a caller
  asserting its own success is recorded as a `caller_assertion`, and the
  firewall's completion gate does not distinguish weights - it requires
  *an* observed success under valid authority, and it is the
  integration's job to make sure that observation is worth anything.
* **In-process integrity is not claimed.** A row edited in place (the
  forged-row tests in `tests/test_v2_8_commit_integrity.py`) violates
  the invariant when the check runs; an adversary already inside the
  process is outside this codebase's threat model, exactly as v2.4
  documented for `canonical_allow_for`.

## 6. Observability

The flight recorder mirrors each journal transition best-effort
(`effect_prepared`, `effect_attempt_started`, `effect_receipt_recorded`
with `outcome` and `authority_valid`, `effect_reconciled` with
`resolution` and `reconcile_count`, `effect_completion_refused`), so an
operator can answer: what did the firewall authorize, what side effect
was intended (by digest), was it started, was an attempt authorized and
with which attempt id, which idempotency key, was a receipt observed and
by whom, was completion established, did authority change during the
process, was the outcome `UNKNOWN`, and was reconciliation attempted.
Observability stays observational: the recorder failures never change a
journal outcome, and state and evidence remain separate journals.

## 7. Release criteria met by this release

The full existing suite, the eight v2.8 files, the strict invariant
suite (19/19 on the exercised estate), thread and two-instance-over-one-
file concurrency tests, crash/recovery tests, retry/idempotency tests,
external-uncertainty tests, and the `side_effect` benchmark group all
ran; every new state transition and external-effect path is either a
protocol method in the census or the documented plain-v7 completion; and
the searches for alternate authorization origins, `UNKNOWN -> SUCCESS`
paths, exception-to-verdict escapes and evidence-as-authority are pinned
by tests rather than prose. See `tests/test_v2_8_crash_matrix.py` for
the attack campaign with its documented expected results, and
`docs/v2.8-performance.md` for the honest price of the stronger
guarantee.

### The final claim

**Agent Firewall cannot guarantee that an uncontrolled external world
will behave transactionally, but it can guarantee that its own
representation of an external side effect never claims more certainty,
authority, or completion than the protocol actually established.**
