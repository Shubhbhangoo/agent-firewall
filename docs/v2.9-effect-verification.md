# v2.9: Effect verification - AUTHORIZED =/= EXECUTED =/= OBSERVED =/=
# VERIFIED =/= COMPLETED

v2.8 made the implicit side-effect boundary explicit, attestable,
idempotent and recoverable, and was careful about one direction only:
a receipt is an observation, never a permission, and `receipt != proof`
unless the integration authenticates the provider. What v2.8
deliberately did **not** decide is whether an observation already in the
journal deserves to be *trusted* for completion. Its `commit_effect`
closed the execution as `COMPLETED` over a succeeded receipt whose
authority flag was valid, whatever the evidence kind - a
`caller_assertion` and an authenticated `provider_evidence` claim
completed identically.

v2.9 draws the separator v2.8 could not:

```text
AUTHORIZED  =/=  EXECUTED  =/=  OBSERVED  =/=  VERIFIED  =/=  COMPLETED
```

> **v2.8 records what happened. v2.9 establishes whether the recorded
> claim can be trusted.**

That is the v2.9 property, and it is the sentence
`EFFECT_VERIFICATION_SOUNDNESS`, the twentieth registered invariant,
machine-checks.

## 1. What verification is, and what it is not

Verification is a new, independently journaled stage. The deployment
supplies the verifier - an authenticator that checks the provider's
status API, a webhook-signature check, an internal auditor - or uses the
built-in structural verifier when none is supplied. Whatever it is, its
verdict is recorded **about the recorded claim, never about the world**:
the firewall cannot know what an uncontrolled external system did. A
`VERIFIED` verdict means the recorded observation survived the check the
deployment configured, and the record says exactly which method
performed that check, so nobody can read more into it than the
deployment actually established.

The built-in structural verifier is deliberately modest. It confirms
only what the firewall's own records can support: the claim is
internally consistent, correctly bound to the exact effect and attempt,
and was observed under currently valid authority. It never returns
`VERIFIED` for evidence that *labels itself* `provider_evidence`,
because a label is not proof - only an authenticating verifier the
deployment wired may confirm provider evidence.

Verification can neither grant authority nor resurrect it:

* Nothing in the verification layer can make an `authorize` allow.
  `FirewallSDK.authorize()` remains the only authorization path
  (`AUTHORIZATION_UNIQUENESS` still pins it), and the verification
  journal writes nothing to the lease journal or the side-effect
  journal.
* A `VERIFIED` verdict is only *recorded* while the execution's
  authority basis still holds. If the verifier returns `VERIFIED` after
  revocation, expiry or a policy/lineage change, the verdict is
  preserved truthfully as `NOT_VERIFIED` naming the loss - never as a
  pass - and the lease is burned exactly as a v2.8 receipt after
  authority loss is burned. Verification never re-authorizes a revoked
  or expired execution.

## 2. The separator: what changed

| Word | Meaning | Who establishes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **AUTHORIZED** | the canonical boundary allowed the request | `FirewallSDK.authorize()` |
| **EXECUTED** | the lease left `STARTED`; the action may have run | the lease record (`executed=True`) |
| **OBSERVED** | an external outcome was reported for one attempt | the side-effect receipt / reconciliation, with its evidence kind |
| **VERIFIED** | the recorded observation was independently checked and held up | a verification claim in the verification journal, bound to the exact effect/attempt/evidence snapshot |
| **COMPLETED** | the execution closed cleanly over established evidence | the lease record, only when the effect is OBSERVED under valid authority **and** VERIFIED with no recorded contradiction |

The v2.9 claims are the two separators v2.8 could not draw:

* **OBSERVED does not imply VERIFIED.** A succeeded receipt - even
  `provider_evidence` - records what a handler claimed. Whether that
  claim can be trusted is a separate question, answered by a separate
  journal.
* **VERIFIED does not imply COMPLETED under lost authority.** The
  completion gate re-establishes the execution's authority basis one
  final time. A verified claim cannot complete a lease whose authority
  was withdrawn, any more than a receipt could.

## 3. What was added

### 3.1 The verification journal (`firewall/effect_verification.py`,
`firewall/verification_store.py`)

A third journal beside the lease journal and the side-effect journal,
one immutable row per claim. A claim's natural id is the digest of its
whole binding:

```text
verification_id = digest(effect_id, attempt_id, evidence-snapshot digest,
                          outcome, method)
```

so every claim speaks about exactly one effect, one attempt, one
evidence snapshot, one verdict and one method - and a record whose
stored fields do not re-derive to its own id is a forged record.
Inserting the identical claim twice returns the existing row (a
crash-safe retry is idempotent); inserting a genuinely different claim -
a contradiction, a verdict on a newer snapshot - stores it beside the
first, so **contradictory evidence is preserved, never resolved by
rewriting**.

The evidence snapshot is re-derived from the side-effect journal row at
verification time (state, observed outcome, evidence kind, correlation
ids, authority flag, observation time), never assembled from caller
strings. A later reconciliation changes the row and therefore the
snapshot, which is exactly what makes an earlier verification stale: the
completion gate trusts only the latest claim about the **current**
snapshot.

The journal is state, not evidence and not authority. An operator may
archive it or throw it away without rewriting the other two journals.
Restart safety mirrors the other journals: rows are written before they
are published in memory, and a SQLite backend
(`SQLiteVerificationJournal`) shares the configured execution/effect
store file, so one restart recovers all three journals from one
database.

### 3.2 The SDK protocol

New methods and parameters:

* **`verify_effect(...)`** - the independent-check step, callable on its
  own. Validates the lease/effect binding, re-establishes the authority
  basis, packages the row's recorded evidence, hands it to the verifier,
  and journals the verdict. Returns a `VerificationResult`; `allowed`
  is true only when a `VERIFIED` claim is on record for the row's
  current evidence.
* **`commit_effect(..., verifier=None, method=None, verifier_note=None)`**
  - the v2.9 COMMIT. Requires the effect `SUCCEEDED` under valid
  authority *and* a current `VERIFIED` claim (with no `CONTRADICTED`
  claim on the same evidence). If no current verified claim exists it
  runs the verifier now - defaulting to the structural verifier, or the
  named one the deployment passed - then completes.
* **`run_effect(..., verifier=None, method=None, verifier_note=None)`** -
  the one-call form now sequences reserve -> start -> prepare -> attempt
  -> handler -> receipt -> verify -> commit.
* **`verification_records()`** - read-only accessor for the journal.

`FirewallSDK(...)` takes `verification_journal=` or
`verification_store_path=`; when neither is given the journal shares the
configured effect/execution store file (or stays in memory).

**Method discipline.** A verifier must name itself. `verifier=None`
means the built-in structural verifier (the only one that may run
unnamed, and the only one that may use the structural method id). A
deployment verifier must be callable, must be passed with an explicit
non-structural `method`, and must return a
`VerifierVerdict` (`VERIFIED` / `NOT_VERIFIED` / `CONTRADICTED`). A
verifier without a method, one claiming the structural id, or one
returning a bare boolean is refused - the journal must never guess whose
check a `VERIFIED` claim came from, and nothing may impersonate the
built-in method.

### 3.3 The completion gate (strict chain)

`complete_execution` refuses a clean `COMPLETED` over an adopted side
effect unless the effect row is `SUCCEEDED` under valid authority **and**
the latest verification claim about its current evidence is `VERIFIED`,
with no `CONTRADICTED` claim recorded against that evidence. A
contradiction cannot be papered over by re-verifying: `CONTRADICTED`
poisons the evidence until the evidence itself changes. Refusals leave
the lease in place for recovery - the gate never guesses and never
rewrites.

### 3.4 What was not built

No second authorization system, no new `authorize` path, no authority
store reachable from the verification journal, and no automatic retry of
an unknown or unverified effect. The verification layer's only effect on
the rest of the system is a refusal.

## 4. The invariant: `EFFECT_VERIFICATION_SOUNDNESS`

The twentieth registered invariant, machine-checked like its
predecessors with three halves:

* **Source census.** Only the declared protocol methods drive the
  verification journal, and only `verify_effect` / `commit_effect` may
  start a verification claim. A second verification path added anywhere
  in the package fails here even if it looks safe.
* **Record hygiene.** Every stored claim re-derives to its own id and
  agrees with its own snapshot; a `VERIFIED` claim speaks about an
  observation recorded under valid authority, and the structural method
  never confirms provider-labelled evidence.
* **Live records.** Every claim names a real effect and the attempt that
  observed it, and a lease recorded `COMPLETED` over an adopted side
  effect carries a current `VERIFIED` claim whose evidence has no
  recorded contradiction - re-derived from the records, so a stale,
  replayed or forged completion cannot hide.

Adversarial coverage lives in
`tests/test_v2_9_effect_verification.py`: forged ids, structural
verification of provider evidence, verification over lost authority,
claims for unrecorded effects or other attempts, and completed leases
whose verification went stale are all reported `VIOLATED`, while the
real protocol - structural for handler/caller observations, a named
authenticator for provider evidence - reports `HOLDS`.

## 5. Honest non-guarantees

Verification does not make the external world transactional, and a
`VERIFIED` claim is not a world guarantee:

* If the deployment wires no authenticator, provider evidence can never
  be `VERIFIED` - the firewall will say so, and the execution will not
  complete. This is a refusal, not a bug.
* A verifier the deployment writes is only as good as its check. The
  firewall records *that* a named method returned `VERIFIED`; it cannot
  certify that the method's check was adequate.
* A crash between the receipt and the verification leaves a row that is
  observed but not verified - exactly what the record says, never a
  guess that verification happened. After a restart the operator runs
  `verify_effect` (or a verified `commit_effect`) over the recovered row.
* The structural verifier's `VERIFIED` establishes internal soundness
  only. Handler and caller observations are faithfully recorded claims;
  confirming them structurally means the record is what it says it is,
  under the authority it says it was under - nothing more.
